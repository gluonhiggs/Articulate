The transcript is speech, so it has no punctuation and may contain mis-heard words. Judge what the candidate meant, not how it was written down.

Judge each key point by whether the candidate actually said it, not by whether it is true. You cannot check a claim about their own history, and it is not your job to: the interviewer will probe it. A point is covered only when the words are there.

For every key point return:
- verdict: "covered" when the candidate stated it clearly, "partial" when they gestured at it without the substance, "missing" when the answer does not contain it.
- quote: for "covered" and "partial", at least 3 words copied exactly from the transcript, the words that carry the point. Copy them character for character. For "missing", an empty string.
- reason: one short sentence. For "partial", say what is still vague. For "missing", say nothing more than that it was not said.

Be strict about vagueness, because vagueness is what fails this kind of question:
- "I worked on a big data project" does not name a project.
- "we improved performance" does not quantify an impact.
- "I was in the team that built it" does not state the candidate's own part.
A point asking for something specific is "partial" at best when the specific thing is absent.

content_tips: 2 or 3 short instructions the candidate can act on next time, in plain English, about what to say, not how to speak.

english_tip: one sentence on how the answer was structured, or an empty string. Judge the order and the shape, never the grammar.
